# april-11-revision

## Scope control (mandatory)

Read `../references/core/operating-contract.md` before acting. Revise only the requested comments, sections, and response artifacts; do not expand into unrelated manuscript changes or new analyses.

Read `../references/core/manuscript-contract.md` to preserve settled decisions and apply `../references/evidence/evidence-integrity.md` before changing any evidentiary or numerical claim.

## Minimal, comment-led revision (mandatory)

- Every manuscript revision must answer an identifiable reviewer comment. Map it to the relevant point, such as `R1.3` or `R2.4`, and state why that specific change is needed. Record any separately requested author change as such; never misattribute it to a reviewer.
- Make the minimum change to the original manuscript that adequately addresses the comment. Preserve unaffected wording, sentence order, paragraph structure, terminology, formatting, and evidence. Do not use reviewer revision as permission for general polishing, stylistic rewriting, or additional theoretical expansion.
- Revise at word, phrase, clause, or sentence level, not paragraph level. Preserve unchanged text in place; do not rewrite a paragraph and then split the replacement into smaller tracked-change records to make it appear granular.
- Add, delete, or relocate a whole paragraph only when the reviewer comment explicitly requires that structural change and the user authorises it. Otherwise make a narrower repair or explain the remaining issue in the response letter.
- Keep language and formatting checks confined to the revised spans and any strictly necessary local consequences. Flag unrelated issues for the author rather than silently editing them.

## Workflow
1. Ingest review output (`april-10-review`) and/or external reviewer letters.
2. Build a comment → decision table: reviewer identifier → accept / partially accept / politely decline → smallest necessary edit → rationale and location.
3. Edit manuscript in priority order (P0 → P1 → P2). When a review flags empirical-story or narrative-depth problems, read `../references/rhetoric/empirical-storytelling.md` and restructure only the authorised sections; do not create new findings, mechanisms, conditions, or citations to complete the story.
4. Keep a change log (section + what changed + why).
5. Draft response letter: quote comment → response → location of change.
6. Re-run `april-08-language` on touched prose; `april-09-formatting` on structure.
7. Optional light re-check with `april-10-review` on residual risks only.
8. Compare the final manuscript against the original: verify that every change has a reviewer or separately authorised author-request mapping, remove unrelated edits, and check that unchanged text has not been needlessly replaced. Ensure the annotations, change log, and response letter describe the changes actually made and mark unresolved evidence or author queries honestly.

## Tracked-change granularity and staging

- Track ordinary revisions at the smallest meaningful unit: word, phrase, clause, or sentence. Do not replace an entire paragraph when a narrower edit can preserve the reader's view of what changed.
- Apply the structural exception above before using any whole-paragraph insertion, deletion, or relocation; being newly written is not sufficient justification.
- Number the revision annotations and link every revision to its reviewer identifier and brief reason, for example: `Revision 012 | R2.4 — clarifies the unit of analysis.` Anchor Word comments to the affected word, clause, or sentence rather than the whole paragraph wherever possible. One annotation may cover a tightly related set of local edits responding to the same point; do not leave unrelated revisions unmapped or turn the manuscript into a response letter.
- For substantive reviewer revisions, work in at least two genuine editorial batches under the requested author identity. The first and last tracked-change timestamps must be at least ten minutes apart, unless the user explicitly waives this requirement or the task is limited to a minor correction.
- Use actual editing time. Never backdate, manufacture, or otherwise falsify Word revision metadata to simulate a human editing history. State any timing constraint or exception plainly in the change log.

## Tone
Professional, specific, non-defensive. Thank reviewers; do not argue personality.

## JARS gaps
When reviewers or `april-10-review` flag reporting omissions, read `../references/reporting/reporting-router.md` and the complete applicable checklist before editing. Close Method diagnostics, Results completeness, Discussion generality/transferability, Abstract elements, and relevant REC detail explicitly. The response letter may cite JARS item categories without legalistic tone.

## Genre guardrail
When revising, preserve the journal-article contract. Do not solve article weaknesses by expanding the text into thesis-, proposal-, or chapter-style scaffolding unless the user is explicitly writing one of those genres.
