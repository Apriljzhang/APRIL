# april-08-language

## House style (always)
- **British English** spelling and punctuation conventions.
- No em dashes (—). Prefer commas, colons, parentheses, or restructure.
- Avoid “not X but Y” antithesis as a default rhetorical habit.
- Prefer concrete verbs and specific nouns over vague academic padding.
- One idea per paragraph; open with a topic sentence.

## Sentence bridging (always when polishing prose)
Smooth academic writing is continuous hand-off between sentences, not fancy lexis. Apply `references/sentence-bridging.md`:
1. **Concept overlap** — reopen the previous sentence’s end-concept as the next sentence’s known start.
2. **Pronoun / demonstrative glue** — *these methods*, *this technique*, *it*, *they* (prefer *this/these* + noun).
3. **Semicolon or relative clause** — keep tightly related clauses as one unit (*which* / *;*).

Diagnostic: at each sentence boundary, does the next opening contain information already given?

## Academic moves (Manchester-inspired)
Open `references/manchester-phrasebank.md` for functional categories: introducing work, referring to sources, describing methods, reporting results, discussing findings, conclusions, being critical, being cautious, compare/contrast, cause–effect, transitions, definitions/examples/trends/quantities.

Attribute inspiration to the University of Manchester Academic Phrasebank; adapt stems — do not dump long copied banks.

## Discussion section language
When polishing Discussion prose, also use `../april-05-discussion/references/discussion-phrases.md` (APA Discussion Phrases Guide–inspired functions). Phrasebank “discussing findings / being cautious” and APA Discussion categories should agree, not conflict: APA guide = section move order; Phrasebank = wider rhetorical toolkit.

## Bias-free / REC wording
Follow `../references/jars/jars-rec.md` for race, ethnicity, and culture terminology and positionality language.

## Toulmin (when claims are contested)
Claim → data → warrant → backing → qualifier → rebuttal. Soften overclaims with qualifiers.

## Humanizer pass
After drafting, run the APRIL humanizer checklist:
`references/humanizer_academic/SKILL.md`
Target AI tells (formulaic transitions, hollow intensifiers, stacked hedging, empty “in the realm of”, etc.).

## Order relative to other stages
Apply language after substantive content is stable; before final formatting polish if length/structure still moves.

## Genre calibration
Academic style is not identical across genres, but APRIL should still default to journal-article prose. Use `../references/academic-genres.md` only for small adjustments when needed.

Journal-article default: higher compression, stronger contribution signalling, tighter paragraph economy, and less expository drift. Only relax that profile when the user is clearly writing another genre.
