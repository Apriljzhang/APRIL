# APRIL — Sentence bridging (inter-sentence cohesion)

Native-sounding academic prose is less about rare vocabulary and more about **no gap between sentences**. Each sentence should hand off to the next: the opening of sentence N+1 should contain information already established in sentence N (given → new).

When polishing a paragraph, inspect every **sentence boundary** and ask: does the start of the next sentence reuse something that already appeared?

## Three bridging techniques

### 1. Concept overlap
Take a concept introduced at the **end** of the previous sentence and reopen it as known background at the **start** of the next.

| Weak (gap) | Stronger (overlap) |
|---|---|
| … different from that seen in stable asthma. Cell counts stay flat in quiet disease. | … different from that seen in **stable asthma**. In **stable asthma**, the total number of cells does not increase. |

Use the same (or a clearly synonymous) term; do not switch labels without signalling.

### 2. Pronoun / demonstrative glue
Bind the new clause to the old one with a clear anaphor: **this / these / that / those** + noun, or **it / they** when the referent is unambiguous.

| Weak | Stronger |
|---|---|
| Many researchers have suggested ways of reducing cost. Reliance on suppliers is high. | Many researchers have suggested **ways of reducing cost**. **These methods** rely on … |

Prefer **this/these + noun** (“these methods”, “this technique”, “this pattern”) over bare **this/it** when several nouns could be the antecedent.

### 3. Semicolon or relative clause (tight pairs)
When two clauses are one continuous thought, do not cut them with a full stop. Link with a **semicolon** or a **relative clause** (*which*, *that*, *who*).

| Cut apart | Bridged |
|---|---|
| … usually takes many hours. This means that the tests are … | … usually takes many hours**; this means** that the tests are … |
| … a protocol with three waves. The protocol was piloted twice. | … a protocol with three waves**, which** was piloted twice. |

Do not force semicolons where the ideas are only loosely related; use a new sentence plus concept overlap instead.

## Editing pass (apply in april-08)
1. Mark each sentence boundary in the target paragraph.
2. If the next sentence opens with brand-new material and no back-reference, apply technique 1, 2, or 3.
3. After bridging, re-check topic sentences still lead the paragraph (bridging is within the paragraph, not a substitute for structure).
4. Keep APRIL house style: British English; no em dashes; avoid stacking empty connectors (“Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally”) as fake bridges.
