# APRIL — Academic phrase moves (Manchester-inspired)

Functional organisation inspired by the University of Manchester [Academic Phrasebank](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/) and the local source PDF `Academic-Phrasebank-Navigable-PDF-2021.pdf`.

APRIL does **not** reproduce the Phrasebank's full wording banks. Use this file as a page-by-page rhetorical map plus an adapted stem bank in APRIL house style. Prefer short, natural stems over stacked templates. Apply the integrated natural-language dynamics in `../SKILL.md`; preserve technical meaning and logical links.

Cross-link for Discussion-only APA moves: `../../april-05-discussion/references/discussion-phrases.md`.

---

## How to use this reference
- Pick the section that matches the rhetorical job of the sentence or paragraph.
- Use the **functions** to diagnose what the prose needs to do.
- Adapt one or two **APRIL stems** into the paragraph; do not paste five stems in a row.
- Keep British English, topic-sentence paragraphs, and proportionate hedging.

## A. Introducing work
**Main job:** move from context to gap to purpose to contribution.

**Functions**
- Show that the topic matters in policy, practice, theory, or social life.
- Summarise what is already known.
- Mark tension, inconsistency, neglect, or uncertainty.
- State the paper's focus, purpose, or research questions.
- Preview the approach when needed.
- Indicate significance, scope, and structure.

**Useful moves**
- Establishing importance
- Reviewing background knowledge
- Opening a research niche
- Stating aims or hypotheses
- Delimiting the study
- Signalling the paper structure

**APRIL stems**
- This issue has become increasingly important in ...
- Recent work has drawn attention to ...
- A growing body of research has examined ...
- However, less is known about ...
- Existing studies have not fully explained ...
- The present study investigates ...
- This paper addresses the question of whether ...
- The analysis focuses on ...
- The study makes two contributions.
- The remainder of the paper is organised as follows.

## B. Referring to sources
**Main job:** position claims in relation to the literature.

**Functions**
- Attribute claims clearly.
- Synthesise converging evidence.
- Mark disagreement or mixed findings.
- Distinguish author-prominent from information-prominent citation.
- Introduce and comment on quotations sparingly.

**Useful moves**
- Reporting what scholars argue
- Summarising established knowledge
- Grouping similar studies
- Noting conflict or debate
- Locating one study within a wider pattern

**APRIL stems**
- Smith (2022) argues that ...
- Several studies have found that ...
- Previous research generally suggests ...
- Findings remain mixed on ...
- There is broad agreement that ...
- By contrast, some authors contend that ...
- Taken together, these studies indicate ...
- As one participant put it, "..." .
- This claim has been challenged by ...

## C. Describing methods
**Main job:** explain and justify how the study was done.

**Functions**
- Name the design or methodological tradition.
- Justify method choice.
- Describe sample, site, corpus, or dataset.
- State inclusion and exclusion criteria.
- Explain instruments, procedures, and analytic steps.
- Note design-stage limitations where relevant.

**Useful moves**
- Justifying a design
- Describing recruitment or data collection
- Describing materials and measures
- Explaining analytic procedures
- Naming software or coding processes

**APRIL stems**
- This study adopted a ...
- A qualitative / quantitative / mixed-methods design was used.
- This approach was selected because ...
- Data were collected from ...
- Participants were recruited through ...
- The sample comprised ...
- Cases were included if ...
- Responses were analysed using ...
- Coding proceeded in three stages.
- All analyses were conducted in ...
- A limitation of this design is that ...

## D. Reporting results
**Main job:** present findings clearly and precisely without interpretation spillover.

**Functions**
- Refer back to the aim or procedure when needed.
- Direct readers to tables or figures.
- Report significant, non-significant, and unexpected findings.
- Compare patterns across groups, variables, or themes.
- Mark section-to-section progression.

**Useful moves**
- Pointing to visual evidence
- Reporting central findings
- Reporting null or weak effects
- Highlighting contrasts or patterns
- Transitioning between findings

**APRIL stems**
- As shown in Table 2, ...
- Figure 3 illustrates ...
- The results indicate that ...
- A significant association was observed between ...
- No meaningful difference was found for ...
- The strongest effect appeared in ...
- A similar pattern emerged for ...
- By contrast, the second model showed ...
- A further finding concerns ...
- Together, these results suggest that ... [use only when still factual]

## E. Discussing findings
**Main job:** interpret the results against literature, mechanism, and boundary conditions.

**Functions**
- Restate a key result in plain language.
- Compare it with prior work.
- Offer an explanation or mechanism.
- Note surprise, complexity, or boundary conditions.
- Mark implications and future directions.
- Hedge interpretations appropriately.

**Useful moves**
- Recasting the result
- Aligning or contrasting with prior studies
- Explaining why a pattern may have appeared
- Stating implications cautiously
- Introducing alternative explanations

**APRIL stems**
- This finding suggests that ...
- One possible explanation is that ...
- The result is consistent with earlier work on ...
- This pattern differs from studies that found ...
- The finding may reflect ...
- Under these conditions, ...
- An alternative interpretation is ...
- These results should be interpreted cautiously because ...
- The findings have implications for ...
- Further work is needed to determine whether ...

## F. Writing conclusions
**Main job:** close with synthesis, significance, and a proportionate final move.

**Functions**
- Return to the central question.
- Summarise the main takeaway selectively.
- State the contribution.
- Note the main limitation briefly.
- Suggest implications for research, policy, or practice.

**Useful moves**
- Re-answering the question with fuller insight
- Drawing out the central contribution
- Signalling limits
- Ending with a grounded forward agenda

**APRIL stems**
- In sum, the study shows that ...
- Taken together, the findings indicate ...
- The paper contributes by ...
- The main limitation concerns ...
- These findings matter because ...
- In practical terms, ...
- Future research should examine ...
- A useful next step would be ...

## G. Being critical
**Main job:** evaluate prior work or claims precisely rather than aggressively.

**Functions**
- Identify conceptual, empirical, or methodological weakness.
- Show where evidence is thin or overstated.
- Distinguish a useful study from a limited inference.
- Offer a more precise alternative.

**Useful moves**
- Noting inadequate evidence
- Questioning a method–claim mismatch
- Identifying unsupported assumptions
- Suggesting improvement

**APRIL stems**
- A limitation of this account is that ...
- This interpretation rests on the assumption that ...
- The evidence for this claim remains limited.
- The analysis does not fully account for ...
- This measure may not capture ...
- A more direct test would require ...
- The argument is persuasive in part, but ...
- The conclusion appears stronger than the evidence warrants.

## H. Being cautious
**Main job:** hedge claims so they remain accurate and credible.

**Functions**
- Soften explanation, implication, or recommendation.
- Limit scope to sample, context, or conditions.
- Avoid over-generalisation.
- Mark possibility rather than certainty.

**Useful devices**
- Modal verbs: *may*, *might*, *could*
- Verbs: *suggest*, *appear*, *seem*, *indicate*
- Framing clauses: *in this sample*, *under these conditions*, *within this context*
- Qualification: *to some extent*, *at least partly*, *is consistent with*

**APRIL stems**
- These findings may indicate ...
- The pattern appears to reflect ...
- This association could be explained by ...
- The result should be interpreted in light of ...
- In this context, ...
- At least in part, ...
- The evidence is consistent with ...
- It would be premature to conclude that ...

## I. Compare and contrast
**Main job:** show similarity, difference, gradation, or exception cleanly.

**Functions**
- Compare studies, groups, periods, or models.
- Mark stronger vs weaker patterns.
- Show contrast without melodrama.
- Track continuity and change.

**Useful moves**
- Similarity
- Difference
- Partial overlap
- Exception
- Relative strength

**APRIL stems**
- Similarly, ...
- In a comparable way, ...
- By contrast, ...
- In comparison with ...
- Whereas the first group ..., the second group ...
- The two accounts overlap in ...
- A key difference lies in ...
- This pattern was more pronounced among ...

## J. Classifying and listing
**Main job:** organise items, types, dimensions, or stages.

**Functions**
- Introduce categories.
- Break a concept into parts.
- Order items by sequence or importance.
- Signal exhaustive vs illustrative lists.

**Useful moves**
- Numbering stages
- Grouping into categories
- Distinguishing major from minor types

**APRIL stems**
- The evidence can be grouped into three categories.
- Two main patterns can be identified.
- The first concerns ..., the second ...
- These factors fall into four broad groups.
- At least three dimensions are relevant here.
- The process unfolded in the following stages.

## K. Defining terms
**Main job:** clarify what a key concept means in this paper.

**Functions**
- Define a term.
- Distinguish one term from related concepts.
- Mark working vs contested definitions.
- Clarify operational use in the paper.

**Useful moves**
- Formal definition
- Working definition
- Conceptual distinction
- Operational clarification

**APRIL stems**
- In this paper, ... refers to ...
- The term ... is used here to describe ...
- This study adopts a working definition of ...
- It is important to distinguish ... from ...
- Unlike ..., ... refers specifically to ...
- For the purposes of this study, ...

## L. Giving examples
**Main job:** make an abstract point concrete without derailing the argument.

**Functions**
- Introduce one example.
- Provide several illustrations.
- Clarify a category or claim.
- Mark representativeness carefully.

**Useful moves**
- Single example
- Non-exhaustive illustration
- Clarifying case

**APRIL stems**
- For example, ...
- For instance, ...
- A clear example is ...
- This can be seen in ...
- One illustration of this is ...
- Such cases include ...

## M. Describing trends
**Main job:** narrate change over time, especially in figures or longitudinal data.

**Functions**
- Describe increase, decrease, fluctuation, stability, and turning points.
- Compare two or more trajectories.
- Highlight peaks, troughs, and recovery.

**Useful moves**
- Rise and fall
- Sharp or gradual change
- Stability
- Reversal

**APRIL stems**
- The rate increased steadily over ...
- A marked decline occurred after ...
- The trend remained relatively stable.
- The sharpest rise was observed in ...
- Levels peaked in ...
- This was followed by a gradual fall.
- By the end of the period, ...

## N. Describing quantities
**Main job:** report amounts, shares, and magnitudes precisely.

**Functions**
- State totals, percentages, fractions, and proportions.
- Compare quantity across groups.
- Mark approximate amounts when exactness is not possible.
- Avoid vague quantity words where numbers are available.

**Useful moves**
- Exact quantity
- Approximation
- Majority/minority
- Relative comparison

**APRIL stems**
- Approximately one third of ...
- Just over half of ...
- Fewer than ten cases ...
- The proportion of ... was higher among ...
- Only a small minority of ...
- By far the largest share ...
- In absolute terms, ...

## O. Explaining causality
**Main job:** describe causes, contributors, mechanisms, and consequences accurately.

**Functions**
- Mark causal vs correlational language.
- Introduce mediators or mechanisms.
- Note enabling, contributing, or constraining factors.
- Avoid overclaiming when the design does not justify it.

**Useful moves**
- Direct cause
- Contributory factor
- Mechanism
- Consequence
- Correlation-only caution

**APRIL stems**
- One factor contributing to ... is ...
- This may help explain why ...
- The change appears to be linked to ...
- The findings are consistent with the view that ...
- These results do not, by themselves, establish causality.
- A plausible mechanism is ...
- In turn, this may lead to ...

## P. Signalling transition
**Main job:** move the reader smoothly across sentences, paragraphs, and sections.

**Functions**
- Add, contrast, sequence, recap, and refocus.
- Open a new subsection.
- Return to an earlier thread.
- Summarise before moving on.

**Useful moves**
- Addition
- Contrast
- Sequence
- Recap
- Return

**APRIL stems**
- Turning now to ...
- A related issue concerns ...
- The next section examines ...
- Before moving on, it is worth noting that ...
- So far, the discussion has shown ...
- Returning to the earlier point, ...
- By contrast, ...
- Taken together, ...

## Q. Writing about the past
**Main job:** handle tense clearly when describing earlier work, past events, or historical context.

**Functions**
- Report what researchers did or found.
- Describe historical developments.
- Distinguish established knowledge from one past study.
- Keep tense choices consistent within a paragraph.

**Useful moves**
- Past simple for completed studies or events
- Present for enduring claims or current paper structure
- Present perfect for research accumulation up to now

**APRIL stems**
- Early studies found that ...
- Researchers in the 1990s focused on ...
- More recent work has examined ...
- At that stage, ...
- The policy was introduced in ...
- Since then, attention has shifted towards ...

## Quick routing by section
- **Introduction:** A, B, H, P
- **Literature review:** B, G, H, I, J, K, L, P, Q
- **Method:** C, H, J, K
- **Results / Findings:** D, I, M, N, P
- **Discussion:** E, F, G, H, I, O, P
- **Conclusion:** F, H, O, P

## House-style overlay
- British spelling: *summarise*, *generalise*, *behaviour* as appropriate to the field.
- No em dashes.
- Topic sentence first; one job per paragraph.
- Prefer concrete verbs over padded nominalisations.
- Use one strong stem, then continue in your own words.
- If three consecutive sentences all begin with a stock transition, rewrite.
