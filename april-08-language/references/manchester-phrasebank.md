# APRIL — Academic phrase moves (Manchester-inspired)

Functional organisation inspired by the University of Manchester [Academic Phrasebank](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/) (John Morley; © University of Manchester). APRIL does **not** reproduce the Phrasebank’s full wording banks. Use categories as rhetorical checklists; adapt short stems into British English house style. Prefer one clean stem over stacked templates. After drafting, run the humanizer.

Cross-link for Discussion-only APA moves: `../april-05-discussion/references/discussion-phrases.md`.

---

## A. Introducing work
**Functions:** establish importance; review what is known; open a niche; state purpose; preview design; claim significance; delimit; outline structure; define key terms.
- Establishing importance (society / discipline / as a problem to be addressed)
- Referring to previous work to establish what is already known
- Identifying controversy, inadequacy, paucity, or a knowledge gap
- Stating the focus, aim, or purpose of the current research
- Describing design/methods in brief; explaining significance; stating scope limits
- Outlining the structure of the paper

**APRIL stems:** This paper examines … / The study investigates … / Prior work has shown … / Less is known about … / The purpose of this study is to …

## B. Referring to sources
**Functions:** report the state of knowledge; synthesise support or contrast; introduce quotations; summarise a review segment.
- Stating what is currently known
- Synthesising supporting vs contrasting evidence
- Author-prominent vs information-prominent citation
- Commenting on a quoted claim (sparingly)

**APRIL stems:** Smith (year) argues that … / Several studies report … / Findings diverge on … / Taken together, these studies suggest …

## C. Describing methods
**Functions:** locate prior methods; justify adoption/rejection; describe sample and criteria; narrate procedures; note methodological limits.
- Indicating methodology; reasons a method was adopted or rejected
- Sample characteristics; inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Process language (purpose, sequence, instruments, questionnaires, statistical procedures)
- Methodological problems or limitations (design stage)

**APRIL stems:** Data were collected using … / Participants were … / Analyses were conducted with … / This approach was preferred because …

## D. Reporting results
**Functions:** reconnect to aims; point to tables/figures; state positive/negative/null results; highlight surprises; report response rates/themes; transition between results; summarise the section.
- Referring back to aims or procedures
- Highlighting significant cells or patterns
- Survey/interview: rates, proportions, themes, participant views, excerpts
- Moving to the next result; comparing two results

**APRIL stems:** As shown in Table/Figure … / Results indicate … / No significant association was observed for … / A further pattern concerns …

## E. Discussing findings
**Functions:** restating a result; marking importance/surprise; comparing with prior work; explaining; cautioning; hypothesising; noting implications; suggesting future work.
(See also APA Discussion functions in april-05.)

**APRIL stems:** This finding suggests … / The result is consistent with … / An alternative explanation is … / These findings should be interpreted cautiously because …

## F. Writing conclusions
**Functions:** restate aims; summarise main findings; significance/contribution; limitations; recommendations for research and for practice/policy.

**APRIL stems:** In sum … / The study contributes … / The main limitation is … / Future research should … / Practically, the findings imply …

## G. Being critical
**Functions:** highlight inadequacies; identify weakness in a study; offer constructive suggestions; critique theory/method; introduce others’ critical stances.
Avoid hostility; prefer specific warrants.

**APRIL stems:** A limitation of prior designs is … / This claim rests on … / A more precise test would …

## H. Being cautious (hedging)
**Functions:** soften explanations, results, implications, recommendations, and futures; avoid over-generalisation.
Devices: *may*, *might*, *suggests*, *appears*, *is consistent with*, *in this sample*, *under these conditions*.

## I. Comparing and contrasting
**Functions:** similarity/difference within or across sentences; disciplined comparison without false antithesis (“not X but Y” as default habit).

## J. Cause and effect
**Functions:** mark causality vs association; contributive language; avoid causal verbs when design is correlational.

## K. Signalling transition
**Functions:** preview sections; introduce/reintroduce topics; move between sections; summarise a section.

## L. Defining, classifying, exemplifying, trends, quantities
**Functions:** define contested terms; classify; give examples; describe trends/high–low points; report quantities precisely.

---

## House-style overlay
- British spelling (*summarise*, *generalise*, *behaviour* as appropriate to field).
- No em dashes.
- Topic sentence first; one job per paragraph.
- Prefer concrete verbs over padded nominalisations.
