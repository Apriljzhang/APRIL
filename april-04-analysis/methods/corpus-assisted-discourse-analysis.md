# APRIL - Corpus-assisted discourse analysis

## When
Use when corpus patterns and close discourse analysis jointly address language-in-use questions. Frequency, keyness, or collocation alone is not discourse interpretation.

## Steps
1. Define the discourse question, corpus boundaries, sampling frame, genres/registers, time period, metadata, comparison corpus, and claim population.
2. Document acquisition, permissions, deduplication, cleaning, segmentation, tokenisation, lemmatisation, tagging, spelling normalisation, and retained exclusions.
3. Assess corpus size, balance, representativeness, and dispersion. Report normalized frequencies with denominators rather than raw counts alone.
4. For keywords, justify the reference corpus, statistic, thresholds, and minimum frequency. For collocation, report span, direction, association measure, frequency threshold, and grammatical constraints.
5. Move iteratively between frequency lists, concordance lines, collocational patterns, texts, genres, and social context. Preserve negative and deviant cases.
6. Use concordance analysis to establish recurring functions and meanings; do not infer stance, ideology, or salience from frequency alone.
7. Test robustness across reasonable preprocessing, subcorpora, dispersion measures, reference corpora, and association settings.
8. Report corpus composition, tools/versions, query syntax, settings, quantitative patterns, representative concordance evidence, exceptions, and the interpretive chain.

## Do not
- Treat absence from a corpus as evidence that a discourse does not exist.
- Conflate frequency with importance or collocation with causation.
- Decontextualize snippets or erase platform, genre, speaker, and temporal differences.
