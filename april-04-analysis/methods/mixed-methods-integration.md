# APRIL - Mixed-methods integration

## When
Use when quantitative and qualitative strands must be integrated to answer a mixed-methods question. Two parallel analyses without integration are not a mixed-methods inference.

## Steps
1. State the mixed-methods question, rationale, priority, timing, and design: convergent, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential, embedded, or another justified variant.
2. Identify where integration occurs: design, sampling, data collection, analysis, interpretation, or more than one point.
3. For convergent designs, compare or merge strands and analyse convergence, complementarity, expansion, silence, and divergence.
4. For sequential designs, show exactly how first-phase findings connect to second-phase sampling, instruments, questions, or analytic focus.
5. If transforming data, explain the quantitising or qualitising rules and check that the transformation preserves relevant meaning and uncertainty.
6. Represent integration with a joint display, linked case matrix, merged narrative, connected model, or another explicit integration product.
7. Assess quality within each strand and then the warrant for the integrated meta-inference. Preserve unresolved contradictions rather than forcing agreement.
8. Report what was learned from integration that neither strand could establish alone.

## Do not
- Present separate quantitative and qualitative sections and call their coexistence integration.
- Let the larger or statistically significant strand automatically overrule the other.
- Hide divergent cases, incompatible constructs, timing mismatches, or unequal data quality.

## JARS
Apply JARS-Mixed and the complete applicable JARS-Quant and JARS-Qual components.
