# APRIL - Online and virtual ethnography

## When
Use for ethnographic inquiry into online cultures, practices, communities, platforms, or linked online-offline fields. The field is constructed through relationships and traces, not assumed to equal one website.

## Steps
1. Define the ethnographic question, entry point, field-building logic, sites/platforms, actors, time span, and online-offline connections to follow.
2. Describe platform affordances, algorithms, visibility, persistence, ephemerality, pseudonymity, and access conditions that shape the data.
3. Record researcher presence, identity presentation, participation, relationships, positionality, and how these influence access and interpretation.
4. Preserve contextual and temporal metadata, link trails, interaction sequences, fieldnotes, capture dates, and an auditable archive of dynamic material where ethically permissible.
5. Combine observation with interviews, participant contact, artefact analysis, or other evidence when motivations and meanings cannot be inferred from traces alone.
6. Make context-specific decisions about consent, privacy expectations, quotation, traceability, vulnerable participants, closed spaces, platform terms, and data security. Public accessibility does not automatically remove ethical duties.
7. Analyse practices and meanings within their sociotechnical setting, including boundaries, absences, migrations, and offline consequences.
8. Report field construction, researcher role, duration/intensity, data generation, ethics, archiving, analytic procedure, and limits of platform- or time-bound claims.

## Do not
- Treat users, posts, or platforms as context-free datasets.
- Quote searchable material in ways that defeat promised anonymity without explicit justification.
- Infer private motives from observation-only data when participant accounts are needed.
