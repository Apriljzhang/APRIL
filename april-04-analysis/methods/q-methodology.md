# APRIL - Q methodology

## When
Use to identify and interpret shared viewpoints through participants' Q sorts. Q methodology uses by-person factor analysis; it is not a survey designed to estimate population prevalence.

## Steps
1. Define the discourse domain, concourse, Q-set construction, participant-set logic, sorting condition, distribution, and post-sort elicitation.
2. Inspect completed sorts and document missing statements, ties, or departures from the sorting protocol.
3. Choose centroid factor analysis or PCA deliberately and justify the choice in relation to the study's abductive and theoretical aims.
4. Retain factors using multiple criteria: eigenvalues, explained variance, number and strength of defining sorts, cross-loadings, stability, interpretability, and theoretical value.
5. State and justify rotation, whether varimax, manual/theoretical, or a transparent combination.
6. Flag defining Q sorts with a consistent threshold and treatment of confounded sorts. Prefer at least two clean defining sorts per factor; three or more gives a safer interpretive base.
7. Construct factor arrays as weighted composite sorts and inspect distinguishing statements, consensus statements, ranks, and contrasts across the whole configuration.
8. Interpret factors holistically with post-sort comments and contextual data. Name viewpoints as accounts, not participant types, and handle bipolar factors explicitly.
9. Report extraction, rotation, retention, flagged sorts, loadings, factor arrays, variance, distinguishing/consensus criteria, and interpretive evidence.

## Do not
- Retain factors from an eigenvalue rule alone.
- Treat Q participants as a representative population sample or factor frequencies as prevalence estimates.
- Interpret isolated high-ranked statements without their relational position in the complete array.
