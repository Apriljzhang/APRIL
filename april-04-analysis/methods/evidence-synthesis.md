# APRIL — Systematic review and evidence synthesis

## When
Use for systematic reviews, scoping reviews, quantitative meta-analysis, qualitative meta-analysis, or mixed evidence synthesis. Design the search and screening workflow in stages 02–03 before synthesis.

## Steps
1. State review type, protocol/registration, eligibility criteria, information sources, complete search dates, screening process, and final study flow.
2. Keep an auditable study matrix with design, sample, context, measures, effect data or qualitative findings, and risk-of-bias judgments.
3. Use a risk-of-bias tool appropriate to included designs; do not replace appraisal with a single generic quality score unless justified.
4. For quantitative synthesis, define the effect-size metric, dependency handling, fixed/common versus random-effects rationale, heterogeneity estimator, and moderator plan before inspecting results.
5. Report study-level and pooled effects with intervals, a forest plot, heterogeneity, outlier and influence diagnostics, moderator analyses, and sensitivity to model and inclusion decisions.
6. Evaluate selective reporting with multiple appropriate diagnostics. Funnel asymmetry is neither necessary nor sufficient proof of publication bias.
7. For qualitative synthesis, use the JARS-Qual QMARS checklist; explain extraction, coding, synthesis, contradictions, context, and interpretive contribution beyond summary.
8. For mixed synthesis, explain how quantitative and qualitative evidence is integrated rather than presenting two disconnected reviews.
9. Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence and calibrate certainty to study limitations and heterogeneity.

## Reporting
Use PRISMA or another review-specific standard required by the field. For quantitative meta-analysis, also apply JARS–Quant Table 9; for qualitative meta-analysis, apply JARS–Qual Table 2.
