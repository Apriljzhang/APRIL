# APRIL — JARS–REC (race, ethnicity, culture)

Adapted from APA Style JARS–REC (2023/2024 tables). Apply **alongside** Quant, Qual, or Mixed for **all** manuscripts. Clarify national/cultural context; treat **culture as distinct** from race and ethnicity; use locally congruent labels.

## Title / author note / abstract
- When REC constructs are focal, reflect them in the title thoughtfully (avoid implying White samples are the unspoken default).
- Author note: precise terminology choices; community partners; **positionality** relevant to this study; CRediT contributions if required.
- Abstract: state when REC is focal; acknowledge sample racial/ethnic heterogeneity; keywords that aid population searchability.

## Introduction / literature (stages 02, 06)
- Select and justify theory; if applying a framework developed for another group, say so and discuss strengths/limits.
- Prefer strengths-/resilience-based framing where appropriate; avoid deficit-only designs that force White comparison groups without theoretical warrant.
- Critique generalizability/transferability of prior evidence for marginalised groups — or acknowledge absence of research.

## Method (stage 03)
- How REC entered the design; who had input (positionality); culturally congruent practices; mistrust/fidelity issues when relevant.
- Transparency and openness practices if used (preregistration, repositories, open materials).
- Population sampled; selection criteria; justify homogeneity when present and link to RQs; representativeness vs representation; oversampling rationale.
- Country of data collection; language(s); tribal/religious identities when salient.
- Detailed sample composition; multiracial/multiethnic handling; recruitment language when targeting specific groups; survey wording/response options for REC; reclassification rules; avoid ethnic gloss — if aggregating, justify how.
- Measure reliability/validity **for groups like the sample**; measurement invariance / cultural validity; justify adaptations and translation/back-translation.
- Recruitment diversity efforts; interviewer/coder REC characteristics when interviews/coding used.
- Secondary data: best available REC variables; birthplace/residence/language when possible; limitations of the dataset’s REC measurement.

## Results / analysis (stage 04)
- Missingness linked to REC; subgroup descriptives when useful (table notes explain coding).
- Analytic choices involving race/ethnicity must follow RQs; sensitivity/weighting when sampling bias is plausible.
- Avoid rote White reference categories; situate comparisons historically/culturally; consider covariates that often covary with race/ethnicity (e.g. SES).
- If race/ethnicity is a predictor/covariate, explain why (avoid racial essentialism; name socioecological proxies) and report variance accounted for — not merely “controlled for.”
- Assess unintended inequitable intervention effects when applicable.

## Discussion (stage 05)
- Contextualise differences (history, structure); name systems of oppression where warranted; avoid over-reading small differences without theory.
- How design shaped participation/diversity/results; how positionality (and interviewer/coder REC) may have shaped interpretation.
- Credit adjacent disciplines (ethnic studies, sociology, public health, etc.) when they already addressed the issue.
- Constraints on generality/transferability; optional “Constraints on Generality” subsection.
- Implications that reach structural barriers, not only individual behaviour change; mitigate misuse/harm risks.

## Citation praxis (stage 02 / 08 / 09)
- Diversify citation; read what you cite; use specialty journals and local-language sources for international work; evaluate citation bias toward privileged “classics.”
