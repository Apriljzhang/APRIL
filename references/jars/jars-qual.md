# APRIL — JARS–Qual (primary qualitative)

Adapted from Levitt et al. (2018), *American Psychologist* (JARS–Qual). Select items that apply. Qualitative Method sections are often longer; iterative designs may combine collection and analysis.

## Title / author note / abstract
- Title: key issues/topic.
- Author note: funding/contributors; conflicts.
- Abstract: problem/objectives; design (participants/data sources); analytic strategy; main findings; implications; **five keywords** (consider method + participants/phenomenon; approach to inquiry only if it aids intelligibility within word limit).

## Introduction
- Frame problem/question and context (case examples/vignettes allowed when helpful).
- Review/critique/synthesise literature: debates, frameworks, barriers, gaps, practical needs.
- Aims/goals; target audience if specific; rationale that the design fits the purpose; approach to inquiry if illuminating; relation to prior publications from the same project if relevant.
- Prefer research questions/goals over NHST-style hypotheses unless the tradition uses them.

## Method
- **Design overview:** data-collection and analytic strategies; approach to inquiry; rationale.
- **Researcher description:** backgrounds/prior understandings; how these were managed and how they shaped collection/analysis (credentials, experience, values, archive choices, etc.).
- **Participants/data sources:** n of people/documents/events; demographics/cultural information/perspectives that could shape data; existing sources; repository info if shared; archival search process if relevant.
- **Researcher–participant relationship:** prior relationships; ethical implications; impact on process.
- **Recruitment:** channels; incentives; ethics/consent/IRB/vulnerable populations; how n was determined; attrition/refusals; rationale to stop (e.g. saturation); purpose as portrayed to participants if different.
- **Selection:** purposive/convenience/theoretical/etc.; inclusion/exclusion; context (when/where).
- **Data collection:** strategies; recording/transformation of data.
- **Data analysis:** procedures aligned with approach to inquiry.
- **Methodological integrity:** enable evaluation of fidelity to the subject matter and utility for goals (data adequacy; perspective management in collection/analysis; groundedness; contextualisation; insight; meaningful contributions; coherence among findings). Describe integrity procedures (e.g. member checks) when used — do not invent empty claims.

## Findings / Results
- Report findings (themes, categories, narratives) and derived meanings.
- Demonstrate analytic process (quotes/excerpts as evidence — not as a substitute for analytic claims).
- Present in a form compatible with design; synthesising illustrations when useful.
- Findings may be combined with Discussion in some traditions; quantified info only if appropriate to goals.

## Discussion
- Central contributions and significance.
- How findings challenge/elaborate/support prior work; how they can be used.
- Similarities/differences vs prior theory/research; alternative explanations.
- Strengths/limitations tied to data quality/sources/analytic processes and methodological integrity.
- Limits of **transferability** across contexts.
- Ethical dilemmas encountered and advice for future work.
- Implications for research, policy, or practice.
- Allow that more than one valid reading may exist.

## Qualitative meta-analysis (QMARS)
If synthesising qualitative literatures: label the meta-analytic form; report literature scope, debates, method (metasynthesis, meta-ethnography, etc.), and search/selection/analysis — see Levitt et al. Table 2 / APA QMARS checklist.
