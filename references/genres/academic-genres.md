# Academic Genre Map for APRIL

APRIL is **for journal articles first**. This note is only a light calibration guide for nearby genres when users explicitly bring them in.

## Core principle

Across genres, strong academic writing still needs:
- a clear problem or question
- a credible evidence base
- a visible line of argument
- fit between claim, method, evidence, and audience

What changes by genre is the **rhetorical contract**: what readers expect you to prove, how much scaffolding they need, and whether the text is judged as a finished contribution or a proposed one.

## 1. Journal article

Default APRIL target.

Typical features:
- compressed, contribution-centred argument
- fast movement from context to gap to purpose
- tight word budgets
- high dependence on journal norms, author guidelines, and reviewer expectations
- abstract, title, introduction, and discussion carry disproportionate weight

Implication for APRIL:
- use the staged workflow as written
- prioritise contribution, economy, section discipline, and submission fit

## Light-touch adjacent genres

Use these notes only when relevant. Do not let them pull APRIL away from article writing.

- **Thesis/dissertation or research-project report**: allow more explicit framing, study architecture, and signposting than a journal article would usually tolerate.
- **Research proposal or grant-style proposal**: write prospectively, stress significance and feasibility, and do not imply completed results.
- **Literature review, essay, or report**: allow more expository synthesis, but still prefer debate-driven organisation over source-by-source summary.
- **Book chapter or book-length project**: keep local section purpose clear and, when needed, signal how a chapter fits the larger work.

## Practical adaptation rules

Before drafting, ask only one question: is this still being judged like a journal article?

- If yes, use APRIL exactly as written.
- If no, make the smallest possible adjustment for proposal, thesis/dissertation, literature-review, or chapter conventions.
