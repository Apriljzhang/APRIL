# APRIL — Problem, purpose, and research questions

Distilled for APRIL from social-science research-writing guidance (notably USC Libraries’ organising guides on the research problem and introduction, and parallel Sacred Heart research-problem materials). Rewrite in the project’s voice; journal templates may override wording.

## Keep three layers distinct

| Layer | Job | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | What is wrong, unclear, contested, or costly in the world/literature? Why does it matter? | Short paragraph |
| **Purpose** | What will *this study* do about that problem (intent, scope, approach)? | 1–3 sentences |
| **RQs / hypotheses** | What, precisely, will be answered or tested? | Numbered list |

Do **not** treat a purpose statement as a thesis (a thesis asserts a claim to be defended). The purpose announces the investigative intent; claims come later from findings.

## Problem statement (ideation output)
A workable problem statement usually:
1. Orients the reader to topic importance (“so what?”).
2. Anchors the RQs/hypotheses that follow.
3. Sets context and **parameters** of what will be investigated.
4. Frames how results will be reported.

Shape: broad area → narrower issue → specific researchable problem. Prefer an evidenced gap (route to `april-02-literature` if unproven). Avoid pure “little research exists” rhetoric.

Types often seen in social sciences (use only if they fit):
- **Descriptive** — portray a situation or set of characteristics.
- **Relational** — association among variables/constructs.
- **Difference** — compare groups, conditions, or times.

## Purpose statement (ideation → framing)
Answer: *What does this study intend to accomplish, for whom/what, by what general means?*

Include, as relevant:
- Intent verb appropriate to design (*examine*, *test*, *explore*, *describe*, *evaluate*, *explain*, *compare*).
- Central phenomenon or variables/constructs.
- Population, setting, or case.
- Optional: method family (survey, interviews, secondary analysis, experiment, fsQCA, …).

### APRIL stems (adapt; British English)
- Quantitative / confirmatory: The purpose of this study is to test whether … among … by examining the relationship between … and …
- Quantitative / comparative: The purpose of this study is to compare … and … with respect to … in …
- Qualitative: The purpose of this study is to explore how … experience … in …
- Qualitative (practice/policy): The purpose of this study is to understand … in order to inform …
- Mixed methods: The purpose of this mixed-methods study is to … [qual aim], to … [quant aim], and to integrate these strands in order to …
- Configurational (e.g. fsQCA): The purpose of this study is to identify configurations of conditions sufficient (and/or necessary) for … among …

**Delimitations (design choices, not post-hoc limitations):** state what the study deliberately will *not* cover and why (feasibility, focus, theory). Save discovered weaknesses for Discussion limitations.

## Research questions
- Open with the problem; let RQs be answerable with the planned design (`april-03-methodology`).
- Align verbs: explore/understand → qualitative; estimate/test/predict → quantitative; integrate → mixed.
- If confirmatory, add hypotheses after RQs (or instead, if the field prefers hypotheses only).
- Mark **primary / secondary / exploratory** aims early (JARS–Quant); keep that tagging through analysis and discussion.

## Hand-off
- Ideation outputs: problem + purpose draft + RQs (± hypotheses) + delimitations note.
- Literature (`april-02`) must be able to evidence the gap named in the problem.
- Framing (`april-06`) polishes purpose into the Introduction; methodology states design fit.
