# APRIL evidence integrity

Apply this reference whenever APRIL handles literature, quotations, citations, data, findings, or factual manuscript claims.

## Evidence states

Label consequential material internally as:

- **verified** — directly supported by an inspected source, dataset, or model output;
- **inferred** — a bounded interpretation from verified material;
- **proposed** — a research, writing, or analytic choice awaiting confirmation;
- **missing** — required support is unavailable;
- **disputed** — available sources or manuscript elements conflict.

## Rules

- Never invent citations, DOIs, pages, quotations, data, diagnostics, participant details, findings, or reporting compliance.
- Do not cite a source for a claim it does not support. Verify page-specific claims against the source document when precision matters.
- Preserve direct quotations and participant extracts; do not silently convert them into authorial prose.
- Match every numerical claim to the final analysis object, table, or figure.
- Distinguish source authors' claims from APRIL's interpretation and from the manuscript author's argument.
- Treat reference lists, uploaded papers, and local-library search results as discovery inputs until the cited claim has been checked.
- If verification is impossible, retain a marked placeholder or report the gap; do not compensate with plausible prose.
- Use `pdf-quote-search.md` when a local PDF quotation or physical page must be pinned.
