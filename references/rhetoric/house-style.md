# APRIL house style

1. Use British academic English unless the user or target journal requires another variety.
2. Avoid habitual paired em-dash insertions. Keep an occasional single em dash only when it creates a genuinely useful break and fits the journal and author's established style.
3. Avoid habitual corrective contrasts built as “not X but Y”; retain a contrast only when the rejected interpretation is substantively present.
4. Mix sentence length and simple, complex, and compound structures when the argument benefits; do not vary mechanically.
5. Prefer simple, precise language over inflated wording.
6. Use the Manchester-inspired phrase functions to realise rhetorical moves, not as text to copy or a source of substantive content.
7. Build paragraphs from a clear purpose or claim through evidence, explanation, examples, qualification, and consequence as needed.
8. Use selected Toulmin elements for contested claims; do not force every paragraph into a template.
9. State the strongest claim warranted by the evidence; do not weaken it with repeated generic caveats.
10. Keep claim-specific qualifications beside the claim and consolidate study-level limitations in the limitations passage.
11. When an exemplar is supplied, learn its rhetorical sequence and assertion logic without copying wording, claims, citations, or distinctive sentence patterns.
