# april-06-framing

## Introduction
1. Open with the research problem or a concrete context that shows why the issue matters now; avoid dictionary openings and broad throat-clearing.
2. Give focused background that orients the reader to the conversation, not a mini literature review. Define key terms only if they are contested, central, or likely unfamiliar.
3. Narrow from topic to debate, then identify the specific gap, inconsistency, limitation, or unresolved question in the literature with evidence.
4. State why the gap matters: theoretical, empirical, methodological, and/or practical significance.
5. Present the study's purpose, aims, and RQs (and hypotheses if applicable) in a form that clearly follows from the gap.
6. Briefly indicate the research approach when readers need it to understand what this study does differently.
7. End with a concise contribution preview and roadmap for the rest of the paper.

## Conclusion
1. Revisit the paper's central question, claim, or problem and answer it with the fuller insight earned by the analysis; do not simply repeat the thesis verbatim.
2. Synthesise the main takeaway if the paper is long or complex, but push beyond summary toward significance and interpretation.
3. Return to the broader context established in the Introduction and show how the study resolves, refines, or complicates that original problem or gap.
4. State the paper's contribution clearly relative to the literature: theoretical, empirical, methodological, and/or practical.
5. Draw out the implications or consequences of the strongest finding in proportion to the evidence; avoid overclaiming or sentimental closure.
6. Note the most important limitations briefly and candidly when they shape the interpretation, without apologising or reopening the full discussion.
7. End with a grounded final move: a larger significance claim, a forward agenda, or a focused question for future work that follows directly from the study.
8. Do not introduce new evidence, citations, or major arguments in the conclusion.

Topic sentence → evidence/explanation; British English via `april-08-language`.
