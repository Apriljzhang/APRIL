# april-12-email

## Scope and default

Whenever the user asks for an email, write a clean, clear, warm email by default. Follow explicit instructions about audience, language, tone, content, and format. This is APRIL's professional correspondence extension; journal word limits, manuscript formatting, and JARS checklists do not apply to emails.

Treat supplied messages and attachments as context. Preserve the user's intended action and factual details. Drafting does not authorise sending.

## Drafting workflow

1. Identify the recipient, relationship, purpose, and requested next action from the prompt or thread. Ask only when a missing detail materially affects the message; otherwise draft with a clearly marked placeholder where necessary.
2. Write the essential message first: purpose or decision, necessary context, and next step. Keep the action visible near the start, usually in the first substantive sentence after the greeting.
3. Add proportionate warmth through a specific acknowledgement, appreciation, consideration for the recipient, or useful support the sender can actually provide.
4. Remove repeated apologies, unnecessary self-justification, stacked softeners, and empty preambles. Keep a sincere apology when responsibility or harm warrants it, followed by an accurate remedy or next step.
5. Check that the recipient can understand what is needed, why it matters, and what happens next without rereading the message.

## Clear, warm language

- Use a subject line that identifies the topic and, where useful, the requested action. Avoid invented urgency.
- State requests respectfully and specifically. A direct question can remain courteous; do not bury it beneath several layers of "just", "maybe", "possibly", or "I was wondering".
- Use acknowledgement and appreciation that fit the actual relationship. A greeting or personal opening can remain when natural, but routine filler should not postpone the purpose.
- Show warmth through practical consideration: a concise summary, a workable option, a real offer of help, or recognition of the recipient's contribution.
- Match formality and directness to the recipient and context. Preserve established names, titles, forms of address, and the sender's voice.
- Use the user's requested language. For English, default to British English unless the thread or user indicates another variety. For Chinese, use natural professional courtesy and match the requested or supplied script rather than translating English formulas mechanically.
- Avoid inflated praise, exaggerated gratitude, adversarial wording, and qualifications that anticipate objections nobody has raised. Retain uncertainty that affects the decision.

## Dynamic length and paragraphs

Let length follow the complexity, relationship, and recipient's information needs. A brief acknowledgement may need only a few lines; a consequential update may need more explanation.

Do not impose a fixed three-paragraph structure, a word-count quota, or similarly sized paragraphs. Give each paragraph one useful job and vary its length naturally. Use bullets when several independent actions, dates, or options become easier to scan. Do not pad a short message or compress necessary context merely to resemble a template.

## Five common scenarios

Use these moves selectively; they are flexible planning aids, not compulsory sentence sequences.

| Scenario | Useful message moves |
|---|---|
| Request approval or a decision | Identify the decision needed; summarise the material point; give a supplied deadline and its practical reason; offer relevant clarification if feasible. |
| Follow up | Name the earlier item and date when known; specify the response or input needed; explain a genuine dependency; state the sender's authorised next step when helpful. |
| Decline a request | Acknowledge the invitation or request; state the boundary clearly; give a brief reason if useful; offer an alternative only when it is real and the sender is willing to provide it. |
| Communicate bad news | State the problem early; explain the known cause and impact; identify what is being done; give a realistic revised timeline or the next update point if supplied. |
| Give feedback | Identify the work and a specific observation; recognise a genuine strength when relevant; name the issue and requested change; give any agreed timing and available support. |

Avoid blaming or diagnosing someone's character when discussing their work. Do not minimise a material issue merely to soften feedback. Do not manufacture praise to follow a feedback formula.

## Factual and commitment checks

- Preserve names, roles, amounts, dates, locations, and the user's intended boundaries. Do not invent deadlines, causes, attachments, approvals, availability, promises, or offers of help.
- Refer to an attachment only when the user supplied or identified it. Do not claim that a file has been attached, a task completed, or a draft sent unless that action was actually performed.
- If an update's timing is uncertain, express that uncertainty once and give the confirmed next step. Do not turn a target date into a guarantee.
- In follow-ups, silence does not imply approval or agreement unless an explicit prior arrangement establishes that rule.
- Avoid creating new commitments through a reassuring closing. Offer support only within the sender's known role and capacity.

## Training and final review

Apply the post's exercises as internal editing checks. Show the exercises only when the user asks for coaching or alternatives.

1. **Essential message, then warmth:** temporarily strip the draft to its factual purpose and action. Confirm the information is complete; then restore natural courtesy and genuine consideration. This exercise does not require deleting an accountable apology from the final email.
2. **Opening-sentence check:** read the first substantive sentence alone. Can the recipient identify the purpose or action? If it sounds like an order, adjust the request or add a relevant acknowledgement while keeping the purpose visible.
3. **Exemplar study:** when the user supplies an effective email, examine how its opening, request, and closing work. Adapt those moves to the new situation while preserving the user's voice and writing original wording.
4. Check clarity, warmth, factual accuracy, commitments, and natural paragraph rhythm. Confirm there is no repeated defensive explanation or formulaic padding.

## Output and email-app actions

Normally provide a subject line and ready-to-use body with an appropriate greeting and closing. If the user requests body-only text or an edit to a passage, return that requested scope. Keep commentary outside the email and include it only when a missing fact or consequential ambiguity needs attention.

Create or modify a draft in an email application only when the user requests that action. Verify the recipient, subject, body, and any attachments by reopening the saved draft before reporting completion. Send only when explicitly authorised for the recipient and purpose, subject to the active tool's approval requirements.
