# APRIL — Discussion phrase functions

Inspired by the APA Style *Discussion Phrases Guide* (7th ed. instructional aid: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/discussion-phrases-guide.pdf). Categories and functions are retained; stems are paraphrased for APRIL British English house style (no em dashes; avoid default “not X but Y” antithesis). Adapt freely; do not paste clumps.

**Tense:** Discussion is usually written in the **present** when interpreting lasting claims; past tense for what the study *did* or *found* is fine in summary sentences.

Also apply `../../references/jars/jars-quant.md` / `jars-qual.md` Discussion items and `../april-08-language/` for hedging and Phrasebank moves.

## 1. Summarising study results
**Function:** Orient the reader before interpretation; restate aims/findings without new analyses.
- The purpose of this study was to …
- The results support the hypothesis that …
- The findings provide evidence that …
- Three findings are central. First … Second … Third …

## 2. Linking findings to previous research
**Function:** Situate results in the literature (consistency, extension, challenge).
- This pattern is consistent with prior work on … (citation).
- These results align with the claim that …
- The present results are consistent with Author et al.’s (year) account of …
- Earlier studies reported … (citations). The present study shows …
- These results offer a direct demonstration of …

## 3. Interpreting results
**Function:** Explain meaning; offer warrants; highlight salient points.
- The findings highlight …
- The results imply that …
- A compelling explanation is that …
- This interpretation is supported by the finding that …
- Two further results merit comment. First … Second …
- One interpretation is that …
- This finding may be explained by …
- Taken together, the findings indicate …

## 4. Limitations
**Function:** Bound confidence; name threats without empty apology.
- At least three limitations deserve note. First … Second … Third …
- One limitation is that … It is possible that …
- Although the results support …, several limitations remain.
- An alternative explanation warrants comment. For example, …
- These limitations can be addressed in future work by …

## 5. Implications
**Function:** State theoretical and/or practical stakes proportionate to evidence.
- Despite these limitations, the results suggest theoretical and practical implications.
- The findings have implications for practice. For example …
- For theory, the results suggest …

## 6. Directions for future research
**Function:** Pose testable next steps (not a wish list).
- The most useful next step is to extend these findings by examining …
- If …, as these results suggest, then research should explore …
- Further work is needed before the extent of … is clear.
- Beyond the points above, several avenues remain open.

## 7. Closing moves
**Function:** Reaffirm contribution without overclaim.
- Despite these limitations, this study is a step toward linking … and …
- Despite these limitations, the study clarifies the relationship between … and …
- The study contributes to evidence that …
- In summary, the research replicates … (citation) and extends it by …
- Although generality must be tested further, the study supports … Furthermore …

## APRIL cautions
- Every literature link needs a real citation the user can verify.
- Match strength of verbs (*indicate*, *suggest*, *imply*) to design strength (see JARS + cautious language in april-08).
- For contested claims, optionally use Toulmin (claim–data–warrant–backing–qualifier–rebuttal).
